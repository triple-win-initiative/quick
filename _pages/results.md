## Key findings from the midline firm survey

The following results come from our midline firm survey, conducted approximately 12 months after program entry in treatment communities.

### Quality improved substantially

The social franchising model improved overall daycare quality by **0.20 standard deviations** relative to control communities—a meaningful shift given baseline conditions.

- **Hygiene and safety** improved by 0.28 standard deviations
- **Availability of toys and manipulatives** improved by 0.19 standard deviations
- **Quality of child experiences** improved by 0.10 standard deviations

The likelihood of at least one child going hungry all day fell by **6.4 percentage points**. Food provision among home-based providers increased by **24 percentage points**—a 62% increase over control.

### Fewer firm closures

Providers in treatment communities were significantly less likely to exit the market. Among home-based centers, the probability of closure fell by **12.8 percentage points**—a **34% reduction** relative to control. Firms that exited tended to score lower on baseline quality measures, suggesting that competitive pressure from franchisees may select toward higher-quality providers over time.

### Spillover effects on competing providers

Even providers in treatment communities who did not enroll in the program showed signs of quality improvement. Among home-based non-enrollees, the child experience index rose by 0.36 standard deviations and food provision more than doubled relative to control. This suggests the social franchising model may create market-wide incentives to improve—not only for direct participants.

### No short-run effects on revenues

Despite the quality gains, we found no statistically significant effects on prices, enrollment, or revenues at midline. This suggests that profitability improvements—if they materialize—may require more time, or may depend on parents gaining better information about which providers offer higher-quality care.

## Endline data collection

We are collecting a second round of firm surveys, household surveys, and child development assessments in mid-2026. These data will allow us to assess longer-run impacts on firm sustainability, maternal labor supply, and children's cognitive and socio-emotional development.

**Results anticipated: 2026.**
